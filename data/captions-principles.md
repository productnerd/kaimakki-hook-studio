# On-Screen Text / Caption Principles (from PDFs)

Sources: ViralTikTokReelsChecklist_Download_.pdf (One Peak Creative), The_Hook_Writing_Guide.pdf (One Peak Creative). 50_Hook_Examples.pdf and 1,000 Viral Hooks (PBL).pdf contain no caption-specific guidance.

- The on-screen text hook must appear in the first 0-5 seconds; nothing matters more than those seconds.
- Text can carry the hook on its own: write the hook as on-screen text to give context to everything else happening in the video.
- Use a text block hook when the video cannot open with someone speaking to camera or a voiceover.
- A text block must linger on screen at least 3 seconds so the viewer can read the entire thing.
- A long text block with a strong hook can lift views because it takes ~15 seconds to read, which extends watch time.
- Text blocks can also clarify a spoken hook or act as a secondary hook layered under the main one (example: headline block "Making my wife PB&J Spaghetti.. 😳😂" over a backstory line "my pregnant wife started putting a 'craving' and 'yuck' list on the fridge").
- Best case: deliver the hook three ways at once: spoken, as an on-screen text block, and in the post caption.
- Keep the hook to one sentence, readable in 3-5 seconds.
- Every hook must be clear and easy to understand, signal the value of the video, and spark curiosity (the Three C's: Clarity, Context, Curiosity).
- Assume nobody knows who you are: the text must state who is involved, what is happening, and hint where it is going (example: "pranking" + "my husband").
- The text should raise a question or hint that a reaction/result is coming; do not resolve it in the text. The payoff belongs in the last 5 seconds.
- Cut the fluff: one extra or missing word can be the difference between 500 and 500,000 views.
- Draft 5 different phrasings of the hook, then eliminate down to one and refine it.
- Add quick-moving subtitles for the whole video to hold attention and avoid missed words; this also makes the content work with sound off.
- Add your own subtitles/captions during editing rather than relying on in-app titles, and check for typos.
- Add graphic "eye candy" (emojis, gifs, b-roll, stock footage) to keep the video moving between text beats.
- For a repeatable series, put a big block title on the thumbnail/cover frame so viewers can find episodes on your feed (example: "Honest Ads" with Honda, Yeti, Tesla).
- As shown in the examples: text hook blocks are 4-9 words, 1-3 lines, centered, on a solid high-contrast box (white box/black text or black box/white text).
- As shown in the examples: emoji go at the end of the line as a reaction cue, 1-2 max (😳😂, 🫣, ⚡).
- As shown in the examples: the spoken hook is mirrored as centered white subtitle text with a dark outline in the lower-middle of the frame.
- Post caption (the description under the video, not an overlay): treat it as prime hook real estate; it should hint at what is coming and spark curiosity about how the video resolves, not be filler or a cute quote (example: "I can't believe her face when she took the first bite!").
- Measure: check the Retention insight 2 days after posting; if over 50% of viewers watch past 5 seconds the hook is working, otherwise rewrite it.
